Game Design Research

The digital games now on the NY Times website maintain a minimalistic look and gameplay that is so permanently established it seems an impossible feat that their creators would manage to get them so right the first time (I imagine there've been minor modifications made, but they've remained largely the same).

Entry 4 - 02.12.26

Visual Thinking Analysis pt. 1

In the Every Picture image exchange, my partner Lexi had photraphed a bag with a clear cover showing their collection of keychains and pins from video games and tv.

The most obvious aspects are of the keychains, especially the bigger and more brightly colored ones. The most interesting and mysterious aspects of the image are all of the semi-hidden pins and keychains that are only found on second glance, be it their position behind other more prominent pins or their desaturated palettes.

Something I had suggested to Lexi in person was to take advantage of the fake software UI printed on the bag as elements to interact with like digital buttons. It would bring attention to the tactility of the keychains and the charm of collecting.

Entry 3 - 02.05.26

Visual Thinking

The first website that came to mind for using visually strong imagery was that of National Geographic. I'd remembered growing up and feeling captivated by Nat Geo magazines with images of beings that seem to tell their own story — a mother bear and her tired cub resting on her back, a cougar mid-jump, a woman bundled in freezing weather.

Images that inspire a user to think invite a sort of interaction with the image and thus the website that wouldn't be achieved with stock imagery— when the user feels they're able to contribute a piece of themselves to a piece of media, they'll feel more of a connection to it and may be more likely to use it, and that's what we want when designing websites.

Though these areas of design seem simple, they're what make or break the usability and quality of a website. I've often decided to switch to a different website for the task I'd hoped to do because the text was unreadable or formatted strangely, and that lends itself to a bad image for the company. If the design of their website is not up to par, I tend not to expect their product or service to be [when concerning larger corporation and not a small business— I'll usualy give them the benefit of the doubt].

While perhaps a strange take, I enjoy Pit Viper for the wackiness of its website. The design looks like something from 15 years ago and there isn't the clean aesthetic most websites use now, but their unique design style comes through very strongly without inhibiting user — and I find that admirable.

Entry 1 - 01.30.26

Overlays Design Pattern

Within the first 10 seconds of opening this article on modals, before getting through the second sentence, I was hit with a modal that took up most of the screen asking for my email to sign up for the author's newsletter... as she said herself, they an be annoying.

Users don't mind modals as long as they've chosen a task that requires it— users do not like being marketed to left and right. It's cumbersome to "x" out of 3 different pop-ups when opening an article that are just ads or self-promotion before I've even gotten a chance to experience their website and content. I imagine pop-ups used to be a effective with older generations of tech-users who take the time to read anything that comes up lest it be important, but younger generations are quick and know when they're looking something useful or not; and there is an increasing intolerance for ads and inauthenticity like never before and there's nothing that'll turn off a young internet user like a website that's inefficient.
