Overlays Design Pattern

Within the first 10 seconds of opening this article on modals, before getting through the second sentence, I was hit with a modal that took up most of the screen asking for my email to sign up for the author's newsletter... as she said herself, they an be annoying.

Users don't mind modals as long as they've chosen a task that requires it— users do not like being marketed to left and right. It's cumbersome to "x" out of 3 different pop-ups when opening an article that are just ads or self-promotion before I've even gotten a chance to experience their website and content. I imagine pop-ups used to be a effective with older generations of tech-users who take the time to read anything that comes up lest it be important, but younger generations are quick and know when they're looking something useful or not; and there is an increasing intolerance for ads and inauthenticity like never before and there's nothing that'll turn off a young internet user like a website that's inefficient.

02.05.26

Visual Thinking

The first website that came to mind for using visually strong imagery was that of National Geographic. I'd remembered growing up and feeling captivated by Nat Geo magazines with images of beings that seem to tell their own story — a mother bear and her tired cub resting on her back, a cougar mid-jump, a woman bundled in freezing weather.

Images that inspire a user to think invite a sort of interaction with the image and thus the website that wouldn't be achieved with stock imagery— when the user feels they're able to contribute a piece of themselves to a piece of media, they'll feel more of a connection to it and may be more likely to use it, and that's what we want when designing websites.
